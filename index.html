Introduction

Structure Becomes Culture

Around 15 BCE, the Roman engineer Vitruvius set down the first surviving treatise on building — ten books arguing that a structure earns its place only when it stands firm, serves its purpose, and pleases the eye. Two thousand years later, that triad still holds. Vitruvia gathers primers, drawings, and case studies across the styles that followed: Roman engineering, Gothic verticality, Renaissance proportion, and the movements built in their shadow.

Whether you're studying for a design history exam or just tracing where a column order came from, this hub is organized the way the discipline itself is: around the same three questions Vitruvius asked first.

About

Why Vitruvia Exists

Architecture is usually taught in fragments — a period here, a landmark there. Vitruvia keeps the throughline: every style on this hub is measured against the same three questions a Roman engineer asked in 15 BCE.

Mission Statement

Built to Last, Like the Structures It Studies

Vitruvia exists to make architectural history legible to students, self-taught enthusiasts, and anyone standing in front of a building wondering why it looks the way it does. We vet every source for accuracy (firmitas), organize the archive so nothing is more than two clicks away (utilitas), and hold every page to the same design standard we ask of the buildings we cover (venustas).
